      How it works, and where it’s weak

      Pressure. A gradient-boosted model estimates win probability before every ball from score, wickets, balls left, required rate, venue par and team strength. Leverage is how far the next ball can move that number. Balls at 1.5× average leverage or more are pressure.

      Performance. Runs over expected, boundary-minus-dot rate, wicket security and win-probability added, each against the average for that over and wickets state. Scores shrink toward zero for small samples. Weaker opposition counts half.

      Limits. Wicket and win-probability measures are noisy: split-half reliability is low, so ball-quality measures carry the most weight. Samples are 80 to 500 pressure balls. Fielding, run-outs and bowler-batter matchups are not modelled.
